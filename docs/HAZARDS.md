# Hazards

What can go wrong with a firmware update, what the kit does about it, what was shown, and what is left.

## What this document is

- A structured list in the style of a hazard analysis: hazard, cause, mitigation, evidence, residual risk.
- The way of working is aligned with ISO 26262 style practice in a loose sense: requirements with IDs, tests traced to them, known gaps written down.
- **It is not a certification, not an assessment, and not a safety case. No ASIL is claimed or implied.** No hazard analysis and risk assessment in the sense of that standard was done: there is no item definition, no severity, exposure or controllability rating, and no independent review.
- The device here is a development board on a bench. What "unusable" means in a real product depends on the product.

About the standards named in this project:

| Standard | Scope, as given in its public catalogue abstract | Used here |
|---|---|---|
| ISO 26262 | Functional safety of safety related electrical and electronic systems in series production road vehicles. Part 6 covers product development at the software level | As a model for the style of this document only |
| ISO 24089:2023 | Requirements and recommendations for software update engineering for road vehicles, on the organizational and the project level. It does not prescribe technologies | Not used. Named because it is the standard about this subject |
| ISO/SAE 21434:2021 | Engineering requirements for cybersecurity risk management for electrical and electronic systems in road vehicles, across their life cycle. It does not prescribe technologies | Not used. The security model in the README is not a threat analysis in its sense |

**Not verified:** none of these standards was read. The scope lines come from search result summaries of public catalogue pages (iso.org entries 68388, 77796, 70918). Nothing in this project was checked against their requirements.

## Hazard table

Evidence refers to [REQUIREMENTS.md](REQUIREMENTS.md) and the sections of [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md).

| ID | Hazard | Cause | Mitigation in the kit | Evidence | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HAZ-01 | A failed update leaves the device unusable | The new image does not start, or starts and does not work | Two slots. The new image runs once in test mode and is confirmed only after the application's health check. Otherwise MCUboot swaps the old image back | OTA-REQ-003, 004. Cases 1 and 2, every test series | The health check is the application's. A shallow check confirms a bad image. The example only checks the LED port and the slot 1 flash |
| HAZ-02 | The device hangs after an update | The new image locks up before or instead of confirming | Watchdog armed before `main()`, fed by the health thread. A reset of an unconfirmed image makes MCUboot revert | OTA-REQ-005. Case 8, reset after 10.1 s in every run | A hang in the SMP thread or below is not detected. A hang after the image was confirmed resets the device again and again, with no way back to the old image |
| HAZ-03 | An image that was not made by the owner boots | Someone sends a modified or foreign image | MCUboot verifies an ECDSA P256 signature at every boot and before every swap | OTA-REQ-001, 002. Cases 3, 4, 4b, 5 | Nothing protects MCUboot or its public key: the debug port is open. See HAZ-07 |
| HAZ-04 | Power is lost during the swap | Supply drops while MCUboot rewrites the slots | MCUboot records the swap progress and resumes. Not something the kit adds | OTA-REQ-030. 49 cuts during the test swap, the revert swap and the confirm: 48 passed, 1 row not valid because of a test script mistake. The power loss tests | One board, one image size, a clean 2 s cut. No brown out, no glitches, no large image. A bad window shorter than about 120 ms could have been missed. The layout still relies on an MCUboot option that is marked experimental. Test supply: 9 V on CN2 VIN with JP4 on 5V_ARD, inside the 7 to 12 V range of UM2153 Rev 5 section 7.4. The ST-LINK USB stayed connected during the cuts, which is not the power order of section 7.5, and 300 mV remained on the 3V3 rail with the supply cut. Both manual references were read from the PDF by the project owner and not checked independently |
| HAZ-05 | Power is lost, or the link drops, during the upload | Supply or host fails mid transfer | The upload goes to slot 1 only. The running image is not touched until MCUboot swaps | OTA-REQ-006, host killed at 50%. OTA-REQ-031: 6 power cuts during an upload and 5 during a slot erase, all passed. The power loss tests | Same limits as HAZ-04. A reset during a flash erase used to stall the boot for 30 s. Fixed and tested, OTA-REQ-008 |
| HAZ-06 | A signing or encryption key leaks | The private key file is copied from the build host, or the encryption key is read from a device | Private keys are not in the repository. The doctor refuses MCUboot's public development keys in release mode | Checks before every push. Doctor fixture `dev_key` | The keys are plain files on one machine. No hardware security module, no key rotation, no way to revoke a key in the field. One encryption key is shared by all devices |
| HAZ-07 | The device is read or rewritten through the debug port | Readout protection is off on this board | None | None | Anyone with a probe can read the firmware and the encryption key, replace MCUboot, and install any version. Every other mitigation assumes this does not happen |
| HAZ-08 | An old, vulnerable version is installed again | Someone sends an older, correctly signed image | MCUboot compares versions and erases an older image | OTA-REQ-007. Case 7 | The check runs at boot, after the upload was accepted. Equal versions not tested, OTA-REQ-032. A probe bypasses it, HAZ-07 |
| HAZ-09 | A stranger takes control of the update channel over Bluetooth | The first device to pair gets the only bond. Pairing does not prove who it is | Encrypted link required. One bond, never overwritten | OTA-REQ-011, 012 | Before the first pairing anyone in range can take the bond. That a second central is refused was not tested, OTA-REQ-033. Whoever holds the bond can upload, erase slot 1 and reset. They still cannot make an unsigned image boot |
| HAZ-10 | Anyone with access to the UART controls the update channel | SMP over UART has no authentication | None in the transport. The signature check still applies | Stated in the README | Upload, erase and reset by anyone on the port. Acceptable on a bench only |
| HAZ-11 | An update is installed while the device is doing something that must not be interrupted | The host sends the reset command, the device reboots at once, and the swap takes 4 to 56 s | None | None, OTA-REQ-034 | The application cannot refuse or delay an update. A product needs a safe state check before the reset |
| HAZ-12 | An image is too large and is refused after it was delivered | The build allowed more than MCUboot can swap | Linker assert with the real limit | OTA-REQ-009 | The guard covers swap using move with sysbuild only |
| HAZ-13 | A configuration mistake makes a protection silently absent | For example MCUboot's watchdog timeout beyond the hardware limit: one log line, and no watchdog | The doctor checks a build for the mistakes found in this project. Build time checks in the kit | OTA-REQ-020 to 024 | The doctor only knows the mistakes it was taught, and knows one SoC's watchdog limit. No FAIL does not mean no mistake |
| HAZ-14 | A test image with a forced failure is shipped | A build option meant for the test suite is left on | Compiler warning in such a build. The image prints what it is. Doctor rule HEALTH-02 | OTA-REQ-023 | It is a warning, not an error. Nothing stops someone from signing and sending it |
| HAZ-15 | The external flash is read or changed with a clip | Slot 1 is on a separate chip | With `ENC=1` the image in slot 1 is encrypted. A changed image fails the signature check | OTA-REQ-013, and case 5 for a changed image | Encryption is off by default and not enforced. Its key can be read from the device, HAZ-07 |
| HAZ-16 | Anyone on the same Wi-Fi network controls the update channel | SMP over UDP has no encryption and no authentication. On the ESP32, port 1337 answers every host that can reach the board. On the STM32 board, the board opens a socket to a host address fixed at build time and serves whoever answers there | None in the transport. The signature check still applies: an image that is not signed with the owner's key does not boot. SMP shell commands are refused once a network is stored | [BOARD_ESP32.md](BOARD_ESP32.md), Wi-Fi results. Upload, slot 1 erase and reset were all done from a laptop that only shared the network. A shell command over UDP was refused with return code 11 | A person on the network can upload a file, erase slot 1, reset the board at any moment, and read which versions are installed. On the STM32 board that person has to take the host's address, or send from it, and the same holds for whoever can reach the relay on the host. They can keep the board busy or keep it resetting. They can see the update in clear text. Downgrade prevention stops an older signed image, nothing stops a replay of the current one. Use only on a network where every device is trusted. DTLS was not looked at |
| HAZ-17 | The Wi-Fi passphrase is read from the board | It is stored in plain text in the settings partition. The ESP32 has no flash encryption here, and the STM32 has readout protection off | None. It is not in the build, the repository or the update image | [BOARD_ESP32.md](BOARD_ESP32.md), "What was built" | Anyone with the USB cable can read the flash, and with it the network name and passphrase. A stolen board gives away the network |
| HAZ-18 | An update image that hangs or aborts before `main()` leaves the device dead | The new image fails in the early start code, before the kit's watchdog is armed. Zephyr's `abort()` halts the CPU. Seen on the ESP32 with an image the build produced without a warning (cache fault in the early flash init, Zephyr issue #120719) | **ESP32:** MCUboot starts the watchdog of timer group 1 with 30 s, feeds it during a swap and leaves it running. The application takes it over. The board's default watchdog does not work for this, it is reset away when the application starts. **STM32:** MCUboot starts the IWDG with 30 s and feeds it during a swap, as it has since the failure handling tests. The IWDG cannot be stopped once started | [BOARD_ESP32.md](BOARD_ESP32.md), the abort before main test and the early hang recovery work. Before: 3 of 3 runs dead until an outside reset. With the mitigation: 2 of 2 runs with the aborting image and 1 run with an image that spins in a `PRE_KERNEL_1` init function, reset by the watchdog 29 s after the hang, old version running 17 s later. STM32, [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) the STM32 early hang test: case 9, 2 of 2 runs, reset by the IWDG 29.8 s after the jump, old version running about 5 s later, no outside help | ESP32: a hang between the application's watchdog driver init and the kit arming it again is not covered. Only Wi-Fi builds were tested. A device that keeps getting the same bad update keeps losing about a minute each time. STM32: tested with a small image and a spinning init function only, an early `abort()` and Bluetooth were not run. Both boards: the hang lasts up to 30 s, during which the device does nothing |

## Largest open risks, in order

1. **HAZ-07, the open debug port.** It undermines the signature check, downgrade prevention and encryption at once.
2. **HAZ-11, no safe state check before an update.**
3. **HAZ-04, power loss during a swap.** Now tested with 60 cuts and no board lost, but only for a small image and a clean cut. Brown out and large images are open.
4. **HAZ-01, the quality of the health check.** The mechanism is tested, the check in the example is shallow.
5. **HAZ-09, HAZ-10 and HAZ-16, who may talk to the update channel.**
6. **HAZ-18, an update that dies before the watchdog is armed.** Mitigated and measured on both boards: MCUboot's watchdog resets the board after about 30 s and the old version returns. Few runs, see the table.
